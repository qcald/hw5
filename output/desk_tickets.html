Ticket #101: Bulldog tee

Expected

The boss reads the board with list_open_tickets and calls inventory first. Inventory uses check_stock to find size-S tees at 0, then list_vendors and get_restock_eta, which show Bulldog Print Co can't ship because invoice 501 is unpaid. The boss then calls accounting, which confirms with list_bills_due that the $840 invoice is overdue and cash covers it. Accounting pays it with pay_invoice once a human approves. The boss goes back to inventory: get_restock_eta now gives an arrival date 5 days out, and it saves a draft vendor order with save_draft. Customer service then uses check_stock and save_draft to draft a reply to Tauhid Zaman with that date. Finally, the boss marks the ticket waiting_on_vendor with update_ticket.

Actual

Final status: waiting_on_human 2 runs 1 approval: $840.00 by Quinn

Run 1: The boss checked the whole board first and called accounting and inventory at the same time. Accounting (list_bills_due, list_payments) reported $3,400.00 in checking, invoice 501 three days overdue, and rent due in 2 days. Inventory (check_stock, list_vendors, get_restock_eta) found 0 size-S tees and reported Bulldog Print Co as the right vendor but blocked by invoice 501, so it gave no arrival date. The boss then had accounting call pay_invoice, which queued an $840.00 payment for human approval and paid nothing. Customer service drafted an honest reply to Tauhid Zaman (out of stock, no date yet), and the boss set waiting_on_human.

Approval and run 2: Quinn approved the payment on the dashboard, and checking dropped to $2,560.00. When the team picked the ticket back up, accounting confirmed the payment in the ledger, and inventory's get_restock_eta now showed the vendor could ship: 1 tee by 2026-09-05 for about $8.00. Inventory saved a draft vendor order, and customer service saved a corrected reply. The ticket ended waiting_on_human rather than the expected waiting_on_vendor, because the agents can only draft orders and a person still has to place it. Customer service also couldn't delete its earlier "awaiting approval" draft, so the ticket holds both versions.

Actual

Final status: resolved 2 runs 1 approval: $2,400.00 by Quinn

Next, facilities used get_lease and list_bills_due to confirm the notice matched lease 1 ($2,400.00 due 2026-09-02, not overdue), and saved a landlord draft that carefully did not claim the rent was paid. Accounting then called pay_rent, which queued the payment for approval, and the boss set waiting_on_human.

It also wouldn't confirm payment in its draft, because it has no ledger tool. The boss relayed accounting's verified facts, and facilities then saved a draft confirming payment 2 and the next due date. The boss marked the ticket resolved. The ticket now holds three landlord drafts, because drafts can't be replaced.

Reflections

How would you evaluate the performance of the agents on each ticket, and why?

Overall, the agents did well on what matters most: accuracy and following the rules. On every ticket they used real numbers from the tools ($840.00, $2,400.00, 0 tees, 8 hoodies, the 2026-09-05 arrival date) and never made anything up. They never claimed a payment had gone through before a person approved it, and they never promised a customer something that wasn't confirmed. Ticket 101 was handled well: the team spotted that the unpaid invoice was what blocked the tee, prepared the payment for approval, and after approval planned the reprint and corrected the customer reply. Ticket 103 showed the best judgment. With no discount policy in the data, the agents left the discount to a person instead of inventing one, and noticed that the restock depended on the same blocked vendor.

The weak spots were efficiency and keeping track of what other agents knew. Ticket 102 reached the right result, but facilities briefly called the new due date a "mismatch" and wouldn't confirm the payment until the boss passed along accounting's ledger check. On 103, accounting wrongly said there were no pending approvals, because it can't see the dashboard's queue. Two smaller costs ran through all three tickets. Tickets 101 and 102 each needed a second run after approval. And because a draft can't be edited, old drafts piled up next to new ones. I'd grade them A on safety and accuracy, B on teamwork and efficiency.

For each ticket, how did Actual compare to the Expected plan?

#101 followed the expected chain closely: stock check, blocked vendor, invoice payment, restock date, vendor draft, customer draft. Two things differed. First, the boss consulted accounting and inventory together before acting, instead of going to inventory first, because its prompt tells it to plan around cash. Second, the ticket ended waiting_on_human, not waiting_on_vendor, because a person still has to place the drafted order. My expected plan also left out that human approval splits the work into two runs. #102 matched almost exactly: the notice was checked against the lease, rent was paid with approval, a landlord draft was saved, and the ticket was resolved at $160.00 left, as predicted. The only surprise was the extra back-and-forth on the second run.

#103 matched the expected outcome: 8 of 20 hoodies in stock, a restock estimated at about $264.00, no discount decided, an "under review" draft, and waiting_on_human. It fell short in one way. I expected accounting to show margins at several discount levels, but it only quoted list price (0% off), because the boss asked whether a discount was allowed, not what different discounts would cost. My expectation that cash couldn't cover the restock after the other two bills also held: $160.00 is less than $264.00.

What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

Honestly, most of this. The shop is small (nine tables, three tickets), and the MCP tools already enforce the hard rules: the overdue math, the blocked vendor, the approval step for payments, and the no-negative-balance check. A single agent with all the tools could have handled each ticket in a handful of calls. Instead, the team re-explained facts in every hand-off, re-fetched the date and ticket in nearly every specialist, and wrote long briefing messages. That cost time and tokens: most runs took 1.5 to 2 minutes for work that is really a few lookups and one payment.

Splitting knowledge across agents also caused the real mistakes. Facilities couldn't see the payment ledger, so on ticket 102 it doubted a payment accounting had already confirmed. On ticket 103, accounting's tools couldn't see the approval queue, so it gave a wrong answer about pending approvals. One agent would have had the full picture the whole time. The team still bought two things: only accounting holds the payment tools, which limits the damage a confused agent can do, and the audit trail reads like a clear conversation. But the server already enforces approval no matter which agent calls it, so a single agent with these tools and a short checklist prompt would likely have been just as safe, cheaper, and less error-prone.

Three new problems this team could solve with the tools we built

1. A weekly low-stock reorder plan. Inventory can run check_stock across every SKU and find the gaps, such as the Crest Mug at 0. It can match each item to a vendor with list_vendors (Elm City Gifts, 3-day lead time) and use get_restock_eta for arrival dates and costs. Then it saves draft orders with save_draft, and accounting checks the total against cash with list_bills_due. 2. A bills-and-cash warning. Accounting and facilities can use list_bills_due, get_lease, and list_payments to spot that October's $2,400.00 rent isn't covered by the $160.00 left. They can draft a heads-up to the owner or the landlord, and if money did come in, prepare the payment for approval when it's due.

3. Group-order quotes for other student organizations. When a club asks for 30 caps or 50 mugs, customer service and accounting can use check_stock and quote_bulk_price at a few discount levels. That gives margins and flags anything below cost. Inventory adds restock dates for any shortfall, and customer service drafts the quote for a person to review. All of this reuses tools we already have. Nothing new would need approval, because no money changes hands.

Three new problems this team could not solve, and what it would need

1. Recording sales and money coming in. If the Yale AI Club pays for its hoodies, nothing can record the sale, add the $1,160.00 to checking, or take the hoodies out of stock. That would need a record_sale tool that adds revenue and lowers inventory together, a refund tool that needs approval, and a Sales agent. 2. Actually placing and receiving vendor orders. Today the agents can only draft orders, which is exactly why ticket 101 stopped short. That would need a place_purchase_order tool that requires approval and checks the budget, and a receive_shipment tool that adds stock and creates the vendor's invoice. A Purchasing agent would own these and follow deliveries against lead_days.

3. Real communication and new requests. The team can't send the drafts it writes or read replies, and it can't turn an incoming email into a new ticket. That would need a send_message tool where a person approves every outgoing message, plus read_inbox and create_ticket tools. A Front Desk / Intake agent would sort incoming requests. Because incoming email is untrusted text, it would also need strong rules against instructions hidden in messages, and a supersede_draft tool so outdated drafts can be retired. A pricing policy table (with a set_discount_policy tool that needs approval) would also let tickets like 103 be resolved without waiting on a person every time.
